% ── Abstract ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
We present the first application of Grassmann manifold sequence mixing
to automatic speech recognition (ASR).  Building on the Causal Grassmann
Layer of~\citet{dong2025attention}, we replace all self-attention in a
Conformer encoder with \plucker{}-coordinate mixing on the Grassmannian
$\Gr(2,r)$ and train end-to-end with connectionist temporal
classification (CTC).  The resulting architecture is \emph{entirely
attention-free}: sequence-level interaction is performed by embedding
pairs of reduced hidden states as two-dimensional subspaces, encoding
their relative geometry via \plucker{} coordinates, and fusing the
result through a learned sigmoid gate.  Local phonetic structure is
captured by depthwise separable convolutions in a Macaron-style
Conformer sandwich.

On LibriSpeech \texttt{train-clean-100}, an 11\,M-parameter model
achieves a validation CTC loss of 0.062 after 29~epochs of training on
a single T4~GPU (\(\sim\)\$6 compute).  Scaling the same 11\,M model to
a nominal 1\,750\,h multi-corpus recipe (LibriSpeech, VoxPopuli,
People's Speech; 385\,K utterances) for 20~epochs yields a held-out
word error rate of 60.7\% (CER 31.6\%) on a 200-utterance subset of
\texttt{test-clean} with greedy character-level CTC, no augmentation
and no language model---a baseline well short of the state of the art.
An independent ESPnet replication at 26\,M parameters with BPE,
SpecAugment and speed perturbation provides a stronger reference: there
the attention Conformer reaches 9.72\%/22.90\% WER on
\texttt{test-clean}/\texttt{test-other}, and the Grassmann variant
trails it by 2.1 points of validation CER after 16 of 70 epochs.
At inference the 11\,M model transcribes 18 seconds of audio in 195\,ms
on a MacBook Air CPU---92$\times$ real-time---without quantisation or
hardware-specific optimisation.

Critically, a controlled ablation replacing \plucker{} coordinates
(antisymmetric products encoding Grassmann geometry) with plain
symmetric products (same dimensionality, no manifold structure)
produces \textbf{equivalent results}: through 29 matched epochs the
validation loss gap never exceeds 0.014, with neither mixer consistently
ahead.  An independent language-modelling replication finds the same
tie.  This indicates that the operative mechanism is the
\emph{fixed-distance multiplicative mixing pattern} rather than the
Grassmann geometry per se.  The symmetric variant additionally trains
2.1$\times$ faster per epoch.

We provide a mean-first reformulation of the \plucker{} mixer that
yields a 3.5$\times$ wall-clock speedup with mathematically identical
output.  All code, trained ONNX models, and training logs are publicly
available.
\end{abstract}

% ── 1  Introduction ───────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The Conformer~\citep{gulati2020conformer} has become the dominant
encoder for end-to-end automatic speech recognition, combining
multi-head self-attention for global sequence modelling with depthwise
separable convolutions for local pattern capture.  However, the
$\mathcal{O}(L^2)$ cost of attention remains a bottleneck for long
utterances and resource-constrained deployment, motivating a search for
attention-free alternatives that maintain comparable accuracy.

Recent work has explored state-space models~\citep{gu2023mamba}, linear
attention variants~\citep{katharopoulos2020linear}, and gated
convolutions~\citep{dauphin2017language} as drop-in replacements for the
attention mechanism.  \citet{dong2025attention} proposed a fundamentally
different approach: replacing self-attention with mixing on the
Grassmann manifold $\Gr(2,r)$, encoding pairs of reduced hidden states
as two-dimensional subspaces via \plucker{} coordinates.  Their Causal
Grassmann Layer achieves competitive perplexity on language modelling
benchmarks with no attention matrices whatsoever.

We present the first application of Grassmann manifold mixing to ASR.
Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \textbf{Grassmann Conformer} that replaces the self-attention
    slot in a standard Conformer block with a two-sided \plucker{} mixer,
    trained end-to-end with CTC loss.
  \item A \textbf{mean-first reformulation} of the \plucker{} mixer
    that is mathematically identical but 3.5$\times$ faster on GPU, reducing
    the number of projection operations from one per offset to one per
    position.
  \item A \textbf{controlled ablation} comparing \plucker{} coordinates
    (antisymmetric products encoding Grassmann geometry) against plain
    symmetric products (same dimensionality, same offsets, no manifold
    structure), isolating the contribution of the geometry from the
    multiplicativeheld-out WER baseline at 1\,750\,h}: the 11\,M
    symmetric-product model trained on 385\,K utterances, evaluated on
    LibriSpeech \texttt{test-clean}, alongside an independent ESPnet
    attention reference at 26\,M parameters.
  \item A \textbf{complete open-source training pipeline} with cached
    mel spectrogram sharding, shard streaming with background prefetch,
    mixed-precision training, incremental checkpoint persistence, and
    browser-based ONNX inference at 92$\times$ real-time on consumer
    hardware.
\end{enumerate}

On LibriSpeech \texttt{train-clean-100} (100\,h, 28\,K samples), our
11\,M-parameter Grassmann Conformer reaches a validation loss of 0.062
after 29~epochs on a single T4~GPU.  Critically, the symmetric-product
ablation tracks nearly identically through all 29 matched epochs,
suggesting that the operative mechanism is the \emph{fixed-distance
multiplicative mixing pattern} rather than the Grassmann geometry per
se.  At 1\,750\,h, the symmetric-product model reaches 60.7\% WER on a
\texttt{test-clean} subset with greedy character CTC---far from
competitive, and a clear marker of how much the recipe (rather than the
mixer) still has to give.  We discuss implications for architecture
 geometry per se.  We discuss implications for
architecture design in~\Cref{sec:discussion}.

% ── 2  Related Work ───────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Attention-Free Sequence Modelling}

The quadratic cost of self-attention has driven extensive work on
sub-quadratic alternatives.  Linear attention~\citep{katharopoulos2020linear}
replaces the softmax kernel with a feature map, reducing complexity to
$\mathcal{O}(Ld^2)$.  State-space models, including
S4~\citep{gu2022s4} and Mamba~\citep{gu2023mamba}, model
sequences through continuous-time linear recurrences with selective
gating.  RWKV~\citep{peng2023rwkv} combines token-shift mixing with
linear-complexity channel mixing.  RetNet~\citep{sun2023retnet} uses
multi-scale exponential decay for retention.  Gated
convolutions~\citep{dauphin2017language} provide another attention-free
primitive, relying on fixed-width kernels with learned gating.

All of these approaches model sequence interaction through either
recurrence, convolution, or linearised dot products.  The Grassmann
mixer of~\citet{dong2025attention} is distinct: it mixes tokens through
bilinear products of coordinate pairs at fixed offsets, with no
recurrent state and no kernel width limitation.

\subsection{Conformer and CTC-Based ASR}

The Conformer~\citep{gulati2020conformer} established that combining
self-attention with convolution in a Macaron-style sandwich yields
state-of-the-art ASR results.  Its architecture---half-step FFN,
multi-head self-attention, ConvModule, half-step FFN, LayerNorm---has
been adopted by ESPnet~\citep{watanabe2018espnet},
WeNet~\citep{yao2021wenet}, and
NeMo~\citep{kuchaiev2019nemo} as the default encoder.

CTC~\citep{graves2006ctc} provides a simple alignment-free loss for
ASR, requiring only an encoder with no autoregressive decoder.  While
attention-based encoder-decoder models often achieve lower WER, CTC
remains competitive when combined with external language models and is
attractive for its streaming capability and architectural simplicity.

\subsection{Grassmann Manifolds in Deep Learning}

The Grassmannian $\Gr(k,n)$---the space of $k$-dimensional subspaces of
$\R^n$---has appeared in deep learning primarily through GrNet for
skeleton-based action recognition~\citep{huang2018grnet} and
Grassmann optimisation for subspace tracking~\citep{edelman1998geometry}.
\citet{dong2025attention} introduced the Grassmannian as a sequence
mixing primitive: pairs of reduced hidden states $(z_t, z_{t+\Delta})$
are embedded as points on $\Gr(2,r)$ via their \plucker{} coordinates,
producing $\binom{r}{2}$ antisymmetric products that encode the relative
geometry of the two-dimensional span.  Their Causal Grassmann Layer
achieves competitive perplexity on WikiText-103 at matched parameter
counts, with no attention mechanism.

% ── 3  Background ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Grassmannian \texorpdfstring{$\Gr(k,n)$}{Gr(k,n)}}

The Grassmannian $\Gr(k,n)$ is the smooth manifold of all
$k$-dimensional linear subspaces of $\R^n$.  For $k=2$, every subspace
$\mathrm{span}(\bm{a}, \bm{b})$ with $\bm{a}, \bm{b} \in \R^r$ can be
represented by its \plucker{} coordinates:
\begin{equation}
  p_{ij} = a_i b_j - a_j b_i, \quad 1 \le i < j \le r,
  \label{eq:plucker}
\end{equation}
yielding a vector $\bm{p} \in \R^{r(r-1)/2}$.  These coordinates are
\emph{antisymmetric}: swapping $\bm{a}$ and $\bm{b}$ negates $\bm{p}$,
so the sign encodes which vector is ``first.''  Normalising $\bm{p}$ to
unit length gives a well-defined embedding of $\Gr(2,r)$ into the unit
sphere in $\R^{r(r-1)/2}$.

For $r=32$, the \plucker{} vector has $\binom{32}{2} = 496$ components,
providing a rich representation of the pairwise geometry between two
reduced hidden states.

\subsection{Conformer Architecture}

The Conformer block~\citep{gulati2020conformer} uses a Macaron-style
layout:
\begin{equation}
  \bm{x} \xrightarrow{+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{FFN}_1}
  \xrightarrow{+\mathrm{Mixer}}
  \xrightarrow{+\mathrm{Conv}}
  \xrightarrow{+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{FFN}_2}
  \xrightarrow{\mathrm{LN}} \bm{y}
\end{equation}
where each FFN is LayerNorm $\to$ Linear $\to$ SiLU $\to$ Dropout $\to$
Linear $\to$ Dropout, the ConvModule applies pointwise convolution,
GLU gating, depthwise separable convolution with batch normalisation,
and a second pointwise convolution.  The \emph{Mixer} slot is typically
multi-head self-attention; we replace it with Grassmann mixing.

\subsection{CTC Loss}

Connectionist temporal classification~\citep{graves2006ctc} defines a
loss over all possible alignments between an encoder output sequence of
length $T'$ and a target label sequence of length $U$, marginalising
over blank insertions.  This eliminates the need for frame-level
alignment supervision and permits a simple encoder-only architecture
with a linear projection to vocabulary logits followed by
$\mathrm{log\_softmax}$.

% ── 4  Method ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Causal Grassmann Mixer}

Given a sequence of hidden states $\bm{h}_1, \ldots, \bm{h}_L \in
\R^d$, the Grassmann mixer proceeds in four steps:

\paragraph{Linear reduction.}  Each $\bm{h}_t$ is projected to a
low-rank representation $\bm{z}_t = W_{\mathrm{red}} \bm{h}_t \in
\R^r$, with $r \ll d$ (we use $r=32$, $d=256$).

\paragraph{Multi-scale pairing.}  For each offset $\Delta \in
\mathcal{W} = \{1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16\}$, we form pairs $(\bm{z}_t,
\bm{z}_{t-\Delta})$.  The pairing is \emph{causal}: position $t$ sees
only past positions.  In our two-sided (encoder) mode, we additionally
pair $(\bm{z}_t, \bm{z}_{t+\Delta})$ with sign flip to distinguish
direction, exploiting the antisymmetry of \plucker{} coordinates.

\paragraph{\plucker{} encoding.}  Each pair is encoded via
\Cref{eq:plucker}, producing a vector $\bm{p} \in \R^{496}$.  The
result is normalised: $\hat{\bm{p}} = \bm{p} / \|\bm{p}\|$.

\paragraph{Gated fusion.}  The encoded representations are projected
back to $d$ dimensions via $\bm{g}_t = W_{\mathrm{plu}} \hat{\bm{p}}_t
+ \bm{b}_{\mathrm{plu}}$, then fused with the input through a learned
sigmoid gate:
\begin{equation}
  \bm{\alpha} = \sigma\bigl(W_{\mathrm{gate}}[\bm{h}_t; \bm{g}_t]\bigr),
  \quad
  \bm{h}_{\mathrm{mix}} = \bm{h}_t + (1 - \bm{\alpha}) \odot
  (\bm{g}_t - \bm{h}_t),
  \label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
following the gating formulation of~\citet{dong2025attention}.

\subsection{Mean-First (Anchored) Reformulation}

The na\"{\i}ve implementation computes \plucker{} coordinates and
projects via $W_{\mathrm{plu}}$ separately for each offset $\Delta$,
then averages the projected outputs.  Since the \plucker{} encoding is
bilinear in $(\bm{z}_t, \bm{z}_\Delta)$ and the subsequent projection
is linear, we can equivalently \emph{average the partner vectors first}
and compute a single \plucker{} encoding per position:
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\bm{z}}_t = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{N}(t)|} \sum_{\Delta \in
  \mathcal{N}(t)} w_\Delta \cdot \bm{z}_{t-\Delta}, \qquad
  \bm{g}_t = W_{\mathrm{plu}} \cdot \mathrm{unit}\bigl(
  \mathrm{pl\ddot{u}cker}(\bm{z}_t, \bar{\bm{z}}_t)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $w_\Delta$ are inverse-norm weights and $\mathcal{N}(t)$ is the
set of valid offsets for position $t$.  This reduces the number of
$W_{\mathrm{plu}}$ matrix multiplications from $|\mathcal{W}|$ to 1,
yielding a measured 3.5$\times$ wall-clock speedup on T4~GPU with
identical output (verified to floating-point precision).

\subsection{Grassmann Conformer Block}

We replace the self-attention slot in the Conformer with a
\textbf{Gated Grassmann Mixer}: the mixer of~\Cref{eq:gate} wrapped
in LayerNorm and dropout, using two-sided mode for the encoder:
\begin{equation}
  \bm{x} \xrightarrow{+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{FFN}_1}
  \xrightarrow{+\mathrm{GatedGrassmann}}
  \xrightarrow{+\mathrm{Conv}_{k=31}}
  \xrightarrow{+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{FFN}_2}
  \xrightarrow{\mathrm{LN}} \bm{y}
\end{equation}

The ConvModule uses kernel size 31, matching the standard Conformer
recipe.  FFNs use SiLU activation with expansion factor 4 ($d_{\mathrm{ff}} = 1024$).

\subsection{Full Model}

The complete encoder-CTC model comprises:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Conv2d subsampling}: two $3 \times 3$ stride-2
    convolutions, reducing the frame rate by $4\times$ (10\,ms fbank
    frames $\to$ 40\,ms encoder frames).
  \item \textbf{Positional encoding}: sinusoidal, following
    \citet{vaswani2017attention}.
  \item \textbf{$N$ Grassmann Conformer blocks}: $N=6$ for the Smol
    configuration.
  \item \textbf{CTC head}: linear projection to vocabulary size 29
    (26 letters + space + apostrophe + blank), followed by
    log-softmax.
\end{enumerate}

The Smol model has 11\,M parameters, consuming 0.2--0.3\,GB of GPU
memory on a T4 (of 16\,GB available).  The architecture is identical
across Grassmann and ablation variants; only the mixer differs.

% ── 5  Experimental Setup ─────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Data}

We train on LibriSpeech~\citep{panayotov2015librispeech}
\texttt{train-clean-100} (100 hours, $\sim$28\,K utterances after
filtering clips shorter than 0.1\,s or longer than 15\,s).  Audio is
resampled to 16\,kHz, converted to 80-dimensional log-mel spectrograms
(400-sample FFT, 160-sample hop), and per-utterance normalised to zero
mean and unit variance.  Transcripts are lowercased with punctuation
removed except apostrophes.

Mel spectrograms are pre-computed and cached as PyTorch tensors in
sharded \texttt{.pt} files, uploaded incrementally to a HuggingFace
Dataset repository.  Training jobs download only the shards needed for
their configured sample count.

\paragraph{1\,750\,h recipe.}  For the scaled run we combine
LibriSpeech~\citep{panayotov2015librispeech},
VoxPopuli~\citep{wang2021voxpopuli} and People's
Speech~\citep{galvez2021peoples} into a nominal 1\,750\,h recipe.  Mel
features are pre-computed with the same front-end and written to 77
\texttt{.tar} shards of 5\,000 utterances each (385\,K utterances).
Roughly 22\% of raw utterances are dropped by the duration filter
($\sim$30\% of VoxPopuli, $\sim$26\% of LibriSpeech, none of People's
Speech), so the effective number of hours is below the nominal figure.
Evaluation uses the official LibriSpeech \texttt{test-clean} split,
which is not part of the training recipe.

\subsection{Baselines}

We compare three mixer variants, all sharing the same Conformer
architecture, parameter count, data, and training recipe:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Grassmann (\plucker{})}: antisymmetric products
    $p_{ij} = z_i z_j' - z_j z_i'$, with flip $= -1$ in two-sided
    mode and Lagrange-identity norm weighting.
  \item \textbf{Symmetric (ablation)}: symmetric products
    $s_{ij} = z_i z_j' + z_j z_i'$, same dimensionality, with flip
    $= +1$ in two-sided mode and symmetric norm weighting.  This
    removes the Grassmann geometric interpretation while preserving
    the fixed-offset multiplicative structure.
  \item \textbf{Attention -CTC (\texttt{egs2/librispeech\_100/asr1})
    with relative-position multi-head self-attention (15 blocks,
    $d=256$, 27.0\,M parameters), trained on \texttt{train-clean-100}
    with BPE-5000 targets, SpecAugment~\citep{park2019specaugment} and
    3-way speed perturbation for 70 epochs on an A100.  The same
    pipeline is run with the attention slot replaced by a Grassmann
    mixer (26.0\,M parameters).  This reference differs from our runs in
    size, tokenisation and augmentation, so it is not a matched
    comparison with the Smol models; it is a matched comparison of
    attention against Grassmann \emph{within} ESPnetocks, $d=256$), trained on the
    same data with the same CTC objective.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Training Details}

All models are trained with AdamW ($\beta_1=0.9$, $\beta_2=0.999$,
weight decay $10^{-2}$), initial learning rate $3 \times 10^{-4}$,
cosine annealing schedule with 3-epoch linear warmup, for 50~epochs.
Batch size is 32.  No data augmentation (SpecAugment) is applied in
the Smol configuration.

Training runs on NVIDIA T4 GPUs (16\,GB) via HuggingFace Jobs.  The
Smol model uses approximately 0.3\,GB of GPU memory.  Checkpoints are
uploaded to

\paragraph{Mixed precision.}  Re-running the Smol-100 ablation with
automatic mixed precision (AMP) reduces epoch time from 542\,s to
$\sim$305\,s (1.8$\times$) with no loss in quality (best validation
loss 0.0209 versus 0.022 in fp32).  All 1\,750\,h training uses AMP.

\paragraph{1\,750\,h training.}  The Smol-1750 symmetric-product model
is trained for 20~epochs with the same optimiser and a 20-epoch cosine
schedule.  Shards are streamed rather than unpacked: the next
\texttt{.tar} shard is downloaded in a background thread while the GPU
trains on the current one, keeping one shard resident in RAM.  Training
is compute-bound at $\sim$1.5\,s/batch on a T4, with shard switches
adding 10--14\% overhead.  The run was interrupted and resumed several
times; resumption restores optimiser, scheduler and AMP scaler state
where available, and otherwise fast-forwards the learning-rate schedule
to the resumed epoch.  Batch size was 128 for the first epochs and 64
thereafter, after 128 exhausted T4 memory on the longest (15\,s)
utterances.  The final resumed job (epochs 8--20) took 38.3\,h of T4
wall-clock time.

Epochs 1--7 were trained on an earlier version of the mel cache that we
later found to contain roughly twice the expected number of samples,
most likely because the recipe was cached twice.  Its internal
validation and test shards therefore probably overlap with training
data (we have not verified this by hashing).  We do not report
internal validation loss for this run; the
only accuracy figure we report is WER on the official LibriSpeech
\texttt{test-clean} split. the HuggingFace Hub after each validation improvement,
enabling automatic resume on job restarts.

\paragraph{Cost.}  The Smol Grassmann model runs at $\sim$1\,123\,s/epoch
(18.7\,min), completing 50~epochs in approximately 15.6~hours of T4
wall-clock time, costing roughly \$6.25 at HF Jobs pricing (\$0.40/hr).
The ablation variant trains at $\sim$542\,s/epoch (9.0\,min), completing
in $\sim$7.5~hours for $\sim$\$3.  Total compute for both models:
under \$10.

\subsection{Decoding}

All reported results use greedy CTC decoding (argmax at each frame,
collapse repeated characters, remove blanks).  No external language
model or beam search is applied, ensuring that all differences
reflect the encoder quality alone.

\paragraph{Matched comparison (epochs 1--29).}
Through 29~epochs under identical conditions, the two variants are
indistinguishable.  The validation loss gap ($\Delta$) never exceeds
0.014 and fluctuates in both directions, with neither mixer
consistently ahead.  At epoch~29 the Grassmann model reached val
loss 0.062 and the Symmetric model reached 0.065---a difference of
0.003 in favour of Grassmann, within noise.

\paragraph{Unmatched comparison (epochs 30+).}
The Grassmann model was resumed from its epoch-29 checkpoint, but
the cosine learning rate schedule reset to epoch~1, causing the LR
to climb back to $3 \times 10^{-4}$ and the validation loss to spike
to 0.092 before recovering.  The Symmetric model ran uninterrupted
with its schedule intact, reaching val loss 0.023 at epoch~47.  These
later epochs are therefore not a fair comparison of mixer quality,
but rather of schedule integrity.

\paragraph{Training speed.}
The Symmetric mixer trains at 542\,s/epoch versus 1\,123\,s/epoch for
the Grassmann mixer---a 2.1$\times$ speedup.  Both models have
identical parameter counts (11\,004\,893).  The speed difference likely
stems from the symmetric norm computation avoiding the cancellation
term in the Lagrange identity used by the \plucker{} norm.

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:ablations}

\paragraph{Geometry vs.\ multiplicative mixing.}
The symmetric-product ablation (\Cref{tab:smol_results}) replaces the
antisymmetric \plucker{} products $a_i b_j - a_j b_i$ with symmetric
products $a_i b_j + a_j b_i$.  This preserves the same number of
output dimensions ($\binom{r}{2} = 496$), the same multi-scale offsets,
the same gated fusion, and the same parameter count.  The only
difference is the sign: the symmetric variant has no Grassmann manifold
interpretation.

The result is unambiguous: \textbf{the two mixers perform
equivalently}.  Through 29 matched epochs, the gap never exceeds 0.014
in validation loss and changes sign repeatedly.  This is consistent
with the independent finding of Vakharwala, who observed a statistical
tie between \plucker{} and symmetric products in language modelling
(WikiText-2, 6 layers, $d=256$, 3 seeds): validation perplexity
330.0\,$\pm$\,0.5 for \plucker{} versus 327.0\,$\pm$\,2.0 for
symmetric, a ratio of 1.009 (95\% CI 0.994--1.025).  In that same
setting, both Grassmann variants trail a matched Transformer
(247.9\,$\pm$\,2.3) by 30--36\%.

This suggests that the operative mechanism in the Grassmann mixer is
the \emph{fixed-distance multiplicative mixing pattern}---pairing
tokens at predetermined offsets and computing bilinear products of
their low-rank projections---rather than the specific antisymmetric
structure that encodes Grassmann geometry.

\paragraph{Training speed.}
The symmetric variant trains 2.1$\times$ faster per epoch (542\,s
vs.\ 1\,123\,s on T4).  The cause is under investigation; both
models have identical parameter counts and the symmetric products
involve fewer operations in the norm computation (no cancellation terms
in the Lagrange identity).

\subsection{Inference Throughput}

The 11\,M-parameter ONNX model transcribes an 18-second audio clip in
195\,ms on a MacBook Air M2 CPU (92$\times$ real-time) using the ONNX
Runtime WebAssembly backend.  No quantisation or hardware-specific
optimisation is applied.  A browser demo is available at
\url{https://tisaconundrum2.github.io/grassman-transcriptions/}.

% ── 7  Discussion ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The geometry question.}
Our ablation provides a clear answer to the question motivating this
work: \emph{Does the Grassmann manifold structure contribute to the
mixer's effectiveness?}  At matched parameter counts and training
epochs, symmetric products perform equivalently to \plucker{}
coordinates.  The useful inductive bias is therefore not the
antisymmetric encoding of two-dimensional subspaces on $\Gr(2,r)$, but
rather the broader pattern of \textbf{fixed-offset multiplicative
mixing}---projecting tokens to low rank, computing bilinear products of
coordinate pairs at predetermined distances, and gating the result back
into the residual stream.

This reframes the contribution of~\citet{dong2025attention}: the
Grassmann manifold provides elegant mathematical motivation for the
mixer design, but the resulting architecture's effectiveness stems from
its structural properties (multi-scale offsets, bilinear interaction,
gated fusion) rather than the specific manifold geometry.

\paragraph{Implications for architecture design.}
If the geometry is not the active ingredient, then practitioners can
use the simpler symmetric-product variant without loss of accuracy,
gaining a 2$\times$ training speedup.  The core design
principle---\emph{pair tokens at fixed offsets and compute bilinear
products of their low-rank projections}---is a viable attention-free
primitive that could be applied to other sequence modelling tasks
(machine translation, language modelling, genomics) independently of
any manifold interpretation.

\paragraph{Attention-free is not yet attention-equivalent.}
The mixer ablation tells us \emph{which} part of the Grassmann layer
matters; it does not tell us whether the layer matches attention.  The
available evidence says it does not yet: in language modelling the
Grassmann layer trails a matched Transformer by about a third in
perplexity, and in the ESPnet ASR pipeline it trails attention by
2.1 points of validation CER mid-training.  The ASR gap is much
smaller than the language-modelling gap, plausibly because the
Conformer's convolution module already supplies local context that
the Transformer language model lacks, but a final number is pending.

\paragraph{Edge deployment.}
The 11\,M-parameter model is small enough for browser-based inference
via ONNX + WebAssembly, achieving 92$\times$ real-time on consumer
hardware.  The absence of attention matrices eliminates the quadratic
memory bottleneck, making the model practical for on-device ASR in
resource-constrained environments.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our current results are limited in several ways:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Weak absolute accuracy}: our best held-out result is
    60.7\% WER on \texttt{test-clean}.  Scaling data from 100\,h to
    1\,750\,h alone did not produce a competitive model at 11\,M
    parameters with our recipe.
  \item \textbf{Partial evaluation}: the 1\,750\,h WER is measured on
    200 of 2\,620 \texttt{test-clean} utterances, with no
    \texttt{test-other} result, and the 100\,h models are compared by
    validation loss only.  The 100\,h \plucker{}/symmetric tie is
    therefore established in CTC loss, not WER.
  \item \textbf{Single seed}: every ASR run in this paper is one seed.
  \item \textbf{Mixer coverage at 1\,750\,h}: only the symmetric mixer
    was trained at 1\,750\,h; we have no \plucker{} or attention run at
    that scale with our pipeline.
  \item \textbf{Cache contamination}: the first seven epochs of the
    1\,750\,h run used a mel cache with duplicated samples, which
    invalidates internal validation for that run.  Held-out
    \texttt{test-clean} is unaffected.
  \item \textbf{Character-level CTC}: we use a 29-token character
    vocabulary with no subword modelling (BPE) and no external language
    model.  Adding beam search with an $n$-gram LM or neural LM would
    likely reduce WER substantially.
  \item \textbf{No SpecAugment or speed perturbation}: neither is
    applied in any of our runs, although both are standard in Conformer
    recipes and both are used in the ESPnet reference.
  \item \textbf{Schedule confound}: the Grassmann model's cosine LR
    schedule reset upon checkpoint resume at epoch~29, making epochs
    30--50 not directly comparable between the two variants.  The
    matched comparison is therefore limited to epochs 1--29, where both
    models ran under identical conditions.
\end{itemize}

% ── 8  Conclusion ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented the first application of Grassmann manifold mixing to
automatic speech recognition, demonstrating that an 11\,M-parameter
attention-free Conformer can learn to transcribe speech from 100~hours
of data with phonetically plausible output.  A controlled ablation
comparing \plucker{} coordinates against symmetric products reveals that
the Grassmann geometry is not the operative mechanism: both variants
perform equivalently, suggesting that the useful inductive bias is the
fixed-offset multiplicative mixing pattern itself.

This finding reframes the architecture's contribution from ``Grassmann
geometry replaces attention'' to ``structured multiplicative mixing of
shifted embeddings is a viable attention-free primitive.''  The
resulting model is simpler to explain, easier to implement, and
deployable in browser-based inference at 92$\times$ real-time.

Scaling the 11\,M symmetric model to 1\,750\,h gives a held-out
baseline of 60.7\% WER on \texttt{test-clean}, which shows how far a
minimal recipe is from competitive.  An independent ESPnet replication
with a standard recipe puts the Grassmann mixer within a few points of
attention CER mid-training; its final WER will be the clearest test of
whether attention-free mixing of this kind is competitive for ASR.

Future work includes adding BPE tokenisation, SpecAugment, speed
perturbation, and beam search with language model fusion to our
pipeline; scaling to 50\,M parameters; and training the \plucker{}
and symmetric mixers side by side at 1\,750\,h.  We also plan to investigate why the symmetric
variant trains 2$\times$ faster per epoch despite identical parameter
counts, and whether the multi-scale offset pattern can be learned rather
than hand-specified.
